The problem

The networks that work are priced for funds.

A hedge fund can pay for a project manager, a roster of former executives, and a bill that arrives at the end of the month. A 40-person company, or a founder, usually cannot. Guidepoint has said it is not built for startups.

01
The bill is the wrong shape.A base fee plus an expert’s hourly rate, prorated to the minute, is a fund product. Operators need a number they can approve before the call.
02
A person still runs the project.Someone takes the request, sources names, screens them, and schedules. That layer is what makes a two-call question feel like a research engagement.
03
The executive is the wrong call.The person 0–4 years into the work knows how it actually gets done. Senior people are allowed. They are not the default.

Trust

The call is about the work.

Rules borrowed from how expert networks already run, written for a practitioner who still has a job.

Not your employer

No confidential information, no material non-public information, and no discussion of your own employer or a direct competitor.

Attest every time

The box is on the invite, not buried in a signup. Decline, or end the call, if the question crosses the line. Ending it still pays in full.

A person approves the send

The agent can draft. It cannot go around the buyer. Caps and a do-not-contact list are part of the request, not a footnote.

Recording is opt-in

A transcript exists only if both sides say yes. The demo never records anything. It only stores the toggle.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Who is Trenchline for?
Small and mid-market companies, and early-stage founders, who need a few expert calls and cannot justify a fund-priced network. The supply is practitioners. The platform is horizontal. Fintech is the vertical we show first.
How is $50 different from Guidepoint and Tegus?
On the November 2026 vendor calls, Guidepoint charged a $150 base fee per shared call plus the expert’s hourly rate prorated to the minute, billed monthly in arrears, and said the product is not built for startups. Tegus charged a $75 transcript fee on top of the expert rate, about $225 for 30 minutes with a $300/hr expert. Trenchline is a flat $50 for 30 minutes, whatever the practitioner’s seniority.
Why practitioners instead of executives?
The person a few years into the seat still does the workflow. Executives are allowed on the platform and usually grade lower when the brief asks for 0–4 years. Use a senior call when the decision is about budget or authority, and say so in the request.
What does the AI project manager actually do?
In the demo it is deterministic JavaScript, with the model hooks marked in the code: draft the ICP, grade candidates, write the outreach, and later draft a call summary. A production version swaps those functions for a model and leaves the approval step where it is. The demo has no API key and sends nothing.
What does a practitioner get paid?
$35 for a completed 30 minutes, prorated to the minute. Nothing if nobody shows. The full amount if they end the call because they were asked to break confidentiality. The demo shows a payout about 15 days later and does not move money.
Do you collect accounts or record calls?
No. The demo keeps example company and profile data in your browser. Don’t type a real person’s details. Recording stays off unless both the buyer and the practitioner opt in, and even then the demo only stores the choice.
